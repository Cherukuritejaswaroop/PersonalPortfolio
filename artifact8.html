Artifact 8

What It Takes to Train a Large Language Model

An infographic that walks through the seven steps of training a generative AI large language model, from data collection to deployment, and compares the data, compute, energy, and time used by models such as GPT-4, Gemini, Llama, Claude, and DeepSeek.

View Infographic (PDF) Open Full-Size Image

Artifact Information

This artifact is a single-page infographic, accompanied by an explanatory document, that illustrates how large language models are trained and what resources the process consumes. Every figure is sourced and labeled as either reported by the developer or estimated by outside researchers.

Introduction

Tools like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini feel instant to use, but each one rests on months of work, trillions of words of text, and thousands of GPUs. This infographic makes those hidden costs visible for a non-technical audience, such as a manager deciding whether to build, fine-tune, or buy access to an AI model.

Description

The graphic opens with four headline numbers, one for each resource category. A numbered timeline then explains the seven training steps: data collection, cleaning and tokenizing, pre-training, supervised fine-tuning, preference alignment, evaluation and safety, and deployment. Under it, a bar based on DeepSeek-V3's reported GPU hours shows that pre-training used about 95.6% of the model's compute.

Four color-coded cards explain data, compute, energy, and time, with examples and why each matters. A comparison table covers GPT-3, GPT-4, Gemini Ultra, Llama 2, Llama 3.1, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and DeepSeek-V3, and a log-scale chart shows estimated training costs rising from about $930 for the 2017 Transformer to about $191 million for Gemini Ultra.

Objective

  • Illustrate the key steps of training a generative AI large language model, from data collection to deployment
  • Explain the four main resource categories (data, compute, energy, and time) and why each matters
  • Compare published training figures for well-known models such as GPT-4, Gemini, Llama, Claude, and DeepSeek
  • Clearly separate developer-reported figures from outside estimates so every number is traceable

Process

  • Reviewed the course resources from IBM Technology and Stephen Wolfram on how large language models work
  • Collected figures from original sources: model cards, technical reports, the Stanford AI Index, and Epoch AI research
  • Labeled each figure as reported or estimated and marked missing figures as not disclosed instead of guessing
  • Calculated comparison values, such as Llama 3.1 using 52 times GPT-3's training tokens, and documented each calculation
  • Designed the layout in HTML with consistent colors, icons, and a log-scale chart, then exported it to PNG and PDF
  • Cross-checked every number against its source before publishing

Tools and Technologies Used

Value Proposition

This artifact shows that I can research a complex, fast-changing topic, verify numbers against original sources, and turn them into a clear visual that helps decision-makers understand cost and risk.

Unique Value: My earlier artifacts focused on how models work and how to test them. This one adds research and cost analysis: it gathers evidence from many sources, judges how reliable each figure is, and presents the trade-offs honestly.

Relevance: Organizations deciding whether to adopt AI need to understand what models cost to build and run. Sourcing data carefully and presenting it clearly is the same skill used in business intelligence reports and technology budgeting.
