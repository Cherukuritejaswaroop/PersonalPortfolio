Artifact 9

Opening the Black Box: Explainable AI

An 11-slide presentation that explains why large language models such as GPT, Claude, Gemini, and Llama are hard to interpret, how validation and performance metrics build trust, and what leading AI labs are doing to make models more transparent, with a hands-on explainability demonstration on a real model.

View Slides (PDF) Download PowerPoint

Artifact Information

This artifact is a slide presentation, accompanied by an explanatory document, about explainable AI (XAI) for large language models. It combines research on industry practice with my own explainability analysis of a breast cancer classification model.

Introduction

AI models now help make decisions in healthcare, finance, and law, but their reasoning is spread across billions of weights and is hard to see. This presentation is written for non-technical decision-makers, such as a hospital administrator or business manager, who need to know when an AI system can be trusted.

Description

The presentation defines transparency, explainability, and interpretability using the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, then presents four challenges: opacity and scale, unfaithful explanations, data quality and bias, and regulation and disclosure. For example, Anthropic found that reasoning models mentioned a hint they used only 25% to 39% of the time.

The middle section shows validation in action. Using the logistic regression model from my model evaluation report (accuracy 0.965, recall 0.925 on 143 test cases), I explained the model globally with permutation importance and locally for one missed cancer that the model scored at 47.6%, just under the 50% cutoff. The final section compares interpretability work at Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, and Meta, and summarizes four explainability approaches and their limits.

Objective

  • Explain the key obstacles in interpreting the outputs of generative AI models
  • Describe the techniques OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Meta use to improve explainability
  • Demonstrate how validation and performance metrics support reliability and transparency
  • Communicate these ideas clearly to a non-technical audience

Process

  • Researched explainability work published by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, Meta, NIST, Stanford, and the EU
  • Defined four separate challenge categories and four solution approaches so each idea has one clear place
  • Re-trained my earlier logistic regression model with the same data, split, and random seed so the results are reproducible
  • Calculated permutation importance and per-feature contributions for one false negative in Python
  • Built the slides with editable PowerPoint charts, matched every source line to the reference list, and checked every number

Value Proposition

This artifact shows that I can evaluate a model with metrics, explain both its overall behavior and its individual decisions, and present those findings in a way that helps non-technical people decide how far to trust an AI system.

Unique Value: My earlier artifacts explained how models work, what they cost, and how to measure them. This one goes a step further by explaining why a model made a specific decision, combining industry research with hands-on explainability analysis.

Relevance: Regulations such as the EU AI Act and frameworks such as the NIST AI RMF expect AI systems to be transparent and explainable. Organizations need people who can audit models and explain their decisions to customers, regulators, and leaders.
